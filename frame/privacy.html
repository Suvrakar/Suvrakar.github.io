Frame Privacy Policy

Frame: AI Video & Audio Editor (Android) · Last updated: 7 November 2026

Frame ("the app") is a video and audio editor for Android, made by Suvrakar. This policy explains what the app does with your information.

In short: your photos, videos, music, recordings and projects stay on your phone. Frame has no account system and no servers that receive your content. Crash reports and usage statistics contain no content or personal details, and you can turn each of them off (section 2). The free version shows ads from Google AdMob (section 3); Frame Pro has no ads. The only time your audio leaves your phone is if you choose an online-only caption language and agree first (section 1).

1. Information the app handles

Information Where it stays Why
Photos, videos and music you add Read from your phone only when you pick them (through Android's photo and file pickers). Never uploaded. Projects keep a link to each file you picked. To edit them and make your video.
Projects (your edits, text, stickers, captions, settings) Stored only on your phone, in Frame's private storage. So you can come back and keep editing.
Exported videos Saved on your phone, in your gallery (Movies/Frame) or in Frame's private storage if you turn "Save to gallery" off. So you can watch and share them.
Microphone audio (only when you record a voiceover) Recorded only while you record, and saved on your phone as part of your project. Never uploaded. To add your voice to the video.
Your video's speech, for auto captions By default, turned into text by your phone's on-device speech recogniser, then discarded. Online languages: some languages can't be recognised on some phones without the internet. Frame then asks you first, and only if you tap Continue is the video's audio sent to Google's speech service to be transcribed, under Google's privacy policy. Frame asks once per language, and you can switch back to an on-device language at any time. To make captions from what is said in your video.
Speech language downloads When you choose a downloadable caption language, Android downloads it from Google onto your phone. So captions in that language can work on your phone without the internet.
Transcript files you import (SRT, VTT or text) Read on your phone and stored with your project. Never uploaded. To show your own captions, timed word by word to the speech.
Phone performance information (chip, memory, encoder limits, temperature and free storage during an export) Used on your phone while it runs. To make exports fast on your phone, warn you if it gets too hot or storage is short, and pause the export before the phone overheats.

Frame does not collect your contacts, location, name, email, or the content of your photos, videos or audio. The advertising ID is used only by Google AdMob to show ads in the free version (section 3), never by Frame itself: Frame's statistics do not use it.

2. Crash reports and usage statistics (you can turn them off)

Crash reports (on unless you turn them off): if Frame crashes or an export fails, the app sends a report to Firebase Crashlytics (operated by Google). A report contains the technical error, the app version, the device model and Android version, free memory and storage, and the names of the last few actions (for example "export started"). Turn off at any time in Settings → Privacy → Send crash reports.

Usage statistics (off unless you turn them on): if you turn on Settings → Privacy → Share usage stats, the app sends a small set of events to Google Analytics for Firebase (operated by Google):

  • which screens you open;
  • when a project is created, and how (blank, template or Magic Movie), and which template or canvas shape was chosen;
  • exports: resolution, frame rate, length, the number of clips, texts and audio tracks, file size, how long it took, whether it failed (with a technical error code), was paused, resumed or cancelled, and the phone's heat level and battery temperature during it;
  • shares: which kind of app you shared to (for example "whatsapp" or "more") and whether Frame prepared a copy for it;
  • Magic Movie: the number of photos and videos used or skipped, the chosen style and length;
  • files your phone could not play (only their type, for example "video", and how many);
  • when a Frame Pro purchase is started or completed;
  • your phone's performance tier (for example "standard" or "lite").

Purchase events never include a price, an order ID or any payment details.

Crash reports and usage statistics never include your photos, videos, audio, captions, text, project names, file names or links. Google may also collect standard technical data such as device model, OS version, app version and approximate country, under Google's privacy policy. Turn either off at any time in Settings, and collection stops immediately.

3. Ads in the free version

The free version of Frame shows ads from Google AdMob (operated by Google): a banner on some screens, and a short video ad only when you choose to watch one to use a Pro feature once. Frame Pro has no ads.

To show and measure ads, Google may collect your device's advertising ID, approximate location from your IP address, and device and app information, under Google's privacy policy and how Google uses information from apps that use its services. Where the law requires it (for example in the European Economic Area, the UK and some US states), the app asks for your consent first through Google's consent form, and you can change your choice at any time in Settings → Privacy → Privacy choices. You can also reset or delete your advertising ID, or opt out of personalised ads, in your phone's settings (Settings → Google → Ads, or Settings → Privacy → Ads).

Frame never gives AdMob your photos, videos, audio, captions, projects or name.

4. Purchases

If you buy Frame Pro (a one-time purchase), payment is handled entirely by Google Play. Frame never sees your card or payment details. It only receives confirmation from Google Play that Pro was bought, to unlock Pro features on your phone.

5. Sharing

Frame does not sell or rent your personal information. Your videos leave your phone only when you share them: when you tap Share, the video goes to the app you choose (for example WhatsApp or Instagram), and that app's privacy policy applies. Otherwise, data leaves your phone only through:

  • crash reports and usage statistics (section 2), which you can turn off;
  • online caption languages, only after you agree (section 1);
  • ads in the free version, which Google AdMob shows (section 3);
  • purchases, which Google Play handles (section 4).

Everything Frame sends is encrypted in transit (HTTPS).

6. Your control and deletion

  • Delete projects and exports: delete any project from the home screen and any export from Exports. Uninstalling Frame deletes all its projects and private files. Videos saved to your gallery stay there until you delete them in your gallery app.
  • Microphone: you can withdraw the permission at any time in your phone's settings. Frame only needs it for voiceovers.
  • Online captions: pick an on-device caption language and nothing is sent.
  • Crash reports and statistics: turn them off in Settings → Privacy at any time.
  • Ads: change your ad consent in Settings → Privacy → Privacy choices (where offered), reset your advertising ID in your phone's settings, or remove all ads with Frame Pro.

There is no Frame account, so there is no data on our side to delete. To remove crash reports or statistics already sent to Google, contact us (section 9); see also how to delete your Frame data. We will ask Google to delete data for your app install where Google supports it. Google keeps crash reports for 90 days and statistics for at most 14 months.

7. Children

Frame is intended for people aged 13 and over and is not directed at children. We do not knowingly collect information from children under 13.

8. Your content and the law

You are responsible for having the rights to the photos, videos and music you use and share. Frame does not see or check your content.
